# `data/results.json` schema

Written by `childcare-100k-build` (`src/childcare_100k/cli.py`). Year keys are strings naming the fiscal year by its start (`"2026"` = 2026-27). Every figure comes from **Microcosm UK 2024-25, the certified national release**, the only dataset.

## Conventions

- **Sign:** costs are positive £bn = extra government spending (or lower receipts). A negative cost would be a saving.
- **Units:** `_bn` = £ billion, 3 dp. `_gbp` = £ a year. `pct_change` and `share_gaining_pct` are **percentages** (0.16 means 0.16%), not fractions.
- **Counts** (families, children) are weighted population estimates rounded to the nearest 1,000. Every weighted figure is computed with microdf.
- **Families** are benefit units (one adult or a couple and their dependent children). Households can hold more than one.
- **Suppression:** a breakdown cell (decile, country) that rests on 1-9 gaining records, or on 1-9 records whose value changes at all (gaining or losing), is published as `null` with `"suppressed": true`. If that leaves a single suppressed cell in a breakdown, the smallest other cell with a nonzero change is suppressed too, so no suppressed cell equals a published total less the published cells; if no second cell can be suppressed, the whole breakdown is withheld. A mean over no gaining families is `null` with `"suppressed": true`, never zero. Record counts are never published.
- **No defaults:** every field below is required. A missing field is a build error, not an empty string or zero.

## Blocks

| Key | Contents |
|---|---|
| `meta` | `policyengine`, `policyengine_uk` (versions run), `policyengine_bundle` (`uk-6.2.1`), `dataset` (`microcosm_uk_2024_25`), `dataset_label`, `dataset_release` (the release's cut tag, `microcosm-uk-2024-25-national-20261002T230158Z-5c6b3f68`), `dataset_repo` (`policyengine/populace-uk-private`), `dataset_revision` (the cut tag's commit, `f9d1922c…`), `dataset_sha256` (`aa31bdf6…`), `dataset_management` (`pinned_by_repo_unmanaged_by_bundle`: this repository pins and verifies the file; the policyengine.py 6.2.1 bundle does not register the release), `years`, `lead_year` (`2027`, an integer), `generated_at` (UTC), `git_revision` (commit the build ran on, so the commit *before* the one that adds the file), `run_provenance` (the provenance key every cached run matched), `min_cell_size`, and `run_seconds` / `peak_rss_gb` per job (each scenario, and `labour_supply`). |
| `reform` | `title`, `description`, `parameters`: the two policyengine-uk parameters, baseline £100,000, reform `"removed"` (set to infinity from 2026-27). |
| `budget.gross_bn` | `thirty_hours`: change in spending on all three funded-hours entitlements (extended, universal, targeted; the universal 15 hours switch off when a family becomes eligible for the extended hours, so the change in extended spending alone would double count). `tax_free_childcare`: change in TFC top-ups. `total` = the sum. Central assumptions. |
| `budget.net_bn.total` | Change in the government balance (minus `gov_balance` change). Equal to gross here: nothing else in the model depends on these limits. |
| `budget.thirty_hours_components_bn` | The `thirty_hours` leg split into `extended`, `universal` and `targeted` (£bn by year; they sum to `thirty_hours`). `universal` is negative: the 3- and 4-year-olds' universal 15 hours switched off as their families move onto the extended entitlement. |
| `budget.range_bn` | `low`, `central` (= `gross_bn.total`), `high`, by year. |
| `budget.sensitivities` | `effects_bn`: each adjustment's effect on the gross total by year (+ raises the cost); `low` / `high`: which effects make up each end; `descriptions`. High: `full_30_hour_usage`, `under_ones` (9-11-month-olds, from the term after they turn 9 months). Low: `ani_net_of_pension_contributions_and_tfc_routed_share`, one joint run, so a family affected by both adjustments is counted once. |
| `recipients[year]` | `families_gaining` (gain > £1 a year), `children_gaining`, `by_scheme` (**families** gaining from each scheme; a family can gain from both), `mean_gain_gbp` (null when suppressed), `mean_gain_suppressed`, `children_by_scheme`, `children_gaining_by_age` (bands `0-1`, `2`, `3-4`, `5-11`, `12+`), `families_losing` (a model artefact: see METHOD.md). |
| `distribution[year]` | `by_decile`: households by baseline equivalised household net income decile (people-weighted, as policyengine-uk's `household_income_decile`), with `mean_change_gbp`, `pct_change`, `share_gaining_pct`, `suppressed`. `by_region` (the twelve ITL1 regions and nations, in ONS order) and `by_family_type` (lone parent; couple with one, two, or three or more children; other): `total_change_bn`, `families_gaining`, `suppressed`, both on benefit units. Nations are regions here, so there is no separate nation breakdown that could reveal a suppressed region by difference. |
| `gender[year]` | `families_gaining_by_earner`: families gaining by which parent is over £100,000 (baseline adjusted net income less returned salary sacrifice): father, mother, both, lone parent; `families_gaining`, `suppressed`, complementary suppression as elsewhere. `partner_not_working`: couples with a child under 12 where exactly one parent is over £100,000, by the sex of that parent: `families`, `partner_not_working_pct` (other parent has no employment or self-employment income). Descriptive and static. |
| `baseline_validation` | Rows (the funded-hours spending comparison with DfE is stated in `limitations`, not here) `label`, `year`, `model`, `official`, `unit`, `source`, `url`, `note`. |
| `assumptions` | Keyed `microcosm`: the model's take-up draws (share of families with `would_claim_*` true, overall and where a parent is over £100,000) and mean extended hours used. |
| `modelling_assumptions` | List of `id`, `title`, `modelled` (what the code models), and for a tested assumption `alternative` and `effect_bn` (by year; the same numbers as `budget.sensitivities.effects_bn`, or for `static` minus `labour_supply.total_offset_bn.central`). |
| `labour_supply` | The labour supply response (METHOD.md, "Behaviour"); every figure keyed `central` / `low` / `high`, then by year. Blocks `extensive`, `intensive` (`at_or_below_limit` and `over_limit` parts). Offsets are £bn, **positive = money back to the Exchequer**, and apply to the total (not split by scheme). `extensive` (moving into work): `offset_bn`, `tax_and_ni_bn` (the offset before the Tax-Free Childcare top-up on entrants' new childcare), `entrants`, `leavers`, `ftes` (to the nearest 100), `earnings_bn`; diagnostics `implied_by_over_limit`, `non_worker_rule_entrants`, `entry_capped` (entry dropped where a non-worker's probability would exceed 1) and `allocated_by_cell_level` (implied entrants placed among non-workers of the same `sex_couple_child_quintile` cell, or, where it has none, of the coarser `sex_couple_child`, `sex_couple` or `everyone` cell; nearest 100); `entry_sensitivity` (sensitivity, central elasticities, by year, not in the dynamic cost): `same_cell_only` (entry that would be placed in a coarser cell dropped) and `worker_profile` (that entry given the implying workers' weighted median hourly wage at 18.8 hours, gain and subsidy recomputed), each with `offset_m` (£m to 0.1), `entrants`, `earnings_bn`; and `central_offset_m`, the central offset at the same precision. `intensive` (hours of every responding adult in work): `offset_bn` (net, the model's tax and benefits recomputed once on the combined earnings change) = `price_offset_bn` (the marginal-childcare-price effect recomputed alone, ≥ 0) + `income_offset_bn` (the OBR income effect, the remainder, ≤ 0), `ftes`, `earnings_bn`; each of `at_or_below_limit` and `over_limit` has the same three offsets, `ftes`, `price_ftes`, `income_ftes`, `earnings_bn`, and diagnostics `workers`, `workers_paying_for_childcare`, `workers_price_falls`, `workers_fully_covered` (thousands; families whose paid care the newly funded hours fully cover), `mean_price_change_pct` (marginal price, among those paying), `mean_income_change_pct` (the household's gain, cash plus paid care displaced by the funded hours less the Tax-Free Childcare top-up on that displaced spend, as a percentage of household disposable income). `intensive_displacement` (sensitivity, central elasticities, both groups): `offset_bn` and `price_offset_bn` by `low`/`high` displacement, and `displacement`. `intensive_income_basis` (sensitivities, central elasticities, both groups, keyed by basis: `paid_care_fixed_spend`, the central gain with childcare spending held fixed, so the top-up on displaced spend is kept; `government_cost`, the gain counting the funded hours at government cost): each `offset_bn`, `price_offset_bn`, `income_offset_bn` by year. `total_offset_bn` = extensive + intensive at the same bound; `dynamic_cost_bn` = `budget.gross_bn.total` − `total_offset_bn`. `population[year]`: `responding_adults` (the participation screen, OBR Table A4), `hours_responding_adults` (the hours screen, Table A3, which keeps disabled adults), `adults_moved_outside_population` (must be 0). `assumptions` (elasticities, including `income_elasticities`; `couples` and `couples_issue_url`, the OBR groups assigned to married and cohabiting couples alike; 18.8 entrant hours, free-hours displacement), `responding_population`, `not_modelled`, `notes`. |
| `cliff_example` | The published cliff family: `description`, `year` (2027), `earnings`, `net_income_baseline`, `net_income_reform` (household net income minus the family's own fixed childcare spending), `notes`. Read from `household_grid`'s default series (no separate simulation). |
| `household_grid` | The dashboard's household form, precomputed with `calculate_household()`: `year`, `region`, `earnings` (the higher earner's employment income: £1 to £140,001 every £1,000, plus every £100 from £95,001 to £105,001), `options` (`parents`, `children`, `spend_per_child`, each `id` + `label`), `default` (the `cliff_example` family, which hits the cliff) and `series` keyed `parent\|children\|spend` with `baseline` and `reform` net income after childcare spending. |
| `benchmarks` | `source`, `figure`, `year`, `like_for_like`, `url`, `announcement_url`, `underlying_source`, `underlying_source_url`. The reported £0.7bn traces to CenTax (free hours only); the comparable figure is CenTax's static £0.98bn (2030) against our `budget.gross_bn.thirty_hours`, given as `ours` with `difference_bn`. |
| `limitations` | Strings. |
