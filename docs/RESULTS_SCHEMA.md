# `data/results.json` schema

Written by `childcare-100k-build` (`src/childcare_100k/cli.py`). Year keys are strings naming the fiscal year by its start (`"2026"` = 2026-27). Every figure comes from **Microcosm UK 2024-25, the national release of 23 October 2026 (a133f37a)**, the only dataset.

## Conventions

- **Sign:** costs are positive £bn = extra government spending (or lower receipts). A negative cost would be a saving.
- **Units:** `_bn` = £ billion, 3 dp. `_gbp` = £ a year. `pct_change` and `share_gaining_pct` are **percentages** (0.16 means 0.16%), not fractions.
- **Counts** (families, children) are weighted population estimates rounded to the nearest 1,000. Every weighted figure is computed with microdf.
- **Families** are benefit units (one adult or a couple and their dependent children). Households can hold more than one.
- **Suppression:** a breakdown cell (decile, country) that rests on 1-9 gaining records, or on 1-9 records whose value changes at all (gaining or losing), is published as `null` with `"suppressed": true`. If that leaves a single suppressed cell in a breakdown, the smallest other cell with a nonzero change is suppressed too, so no suppressed cell equals a published total less the published cells; if no second cell can be suppressed, the whole breakdown is withheld. A mean over no gaining families is `null` with `"suppressed": true`, never zero. Record counts are never published.
- **No defaults:** every field below is required. A missing field is a build error, not an empty string or zero.

## Blocks

| Key | Contents |
|---|---|
| `meta` | `policyengine`, `policyengine_uk` (versions run), `policyengine_bundle` (`uk-6.10.0`), `policyengine_bundle_model_version` (the policyengine-uk version that bundle carries, `2.132.6`; the runs use 2.147.2, which the release pins), `dataset` (`microcosm_uk_2024_25`), `dataset_label`, `dataset_release` (the release's cut tag, `microcosm-uk-2024-25-national-20261023T075039Z-a133f37a`), `dataset_repo` (`policyengine/populace-uk-private`), `dataset_revision` (the cut tag's commit, `c3a751d9…`), `dataset_sha256` (`64f83900…`), `dataset_management` (`pinned_by_repo_unmanaged_by_bundle`: this repository pins and verifies the file; no policyengine.py bundle registers the release yet), `dataset_status` (in words), `years`, `lead_year` (`2027`, an integer), `generated_at` (UTC), `git_revision` (commit the build ran on, so the commit *before* the one that adds the file; the build refuses uncommitted changes under `src/`), `aggregation_sha256` (hash of the aggregation code: `aggregate.py`, `cli.py`, `config.py`, `household.py`, `validation.py`), `run_provenance` (the provenance key every cached run matched), `min_cell_size`, and `run_seconds` / `peak_rss_gb` per job (each scenario, the expected-mode pair `baseline_claim_all` / `reform_claim_all`, and `labour_supply`). |
| `reform` | `title`, `description`, `parameters`: the two policyengine-uk parameters, baseline £100,000, reform `"removed"` (set to infinity from 2026-27). |
| `budget.gross_bn` | `thirty_hours`: change in spending on all three funded-hours entitlements (extended, universal, targeted; summed so nothing is missed if the universal or targeted hours move). `tax_free_childcare`: change in TFC top-ups, with each child's new funded hours netted off its childcare spending in the reform. `total` = the sum. Central assumptions, on the expected basis: the 30 hours averaged over the take-up draw at the contract rate (`thirty_hours_take_up.rate`), Tax-Free Childcare weighting the netted and un-netted top-up by the same rate (METHOD.md, Expected mode). |
| `budget.single_draw_bn` | `thirty_hours`, `tax_free_childcare`, `total` on the dataset's single take-up draw (the central pair as drawn). The breakdowns, the sensitivities' effects and the labour supply offsets are measured on this basis. |
| `budget.thirty_hours_take_up` | `rate` (the take-up contract's working-parent rate, 0.8361), `rate_source_url`, `per_point_m[year]`: £m change in the 30 hours per 0.01 of the rate. |
| `budget.uncertainty` | Bootstrap over FRS source households (`draws`, 2,000; `seed`; `method`; `reproduces_url`). `by_year` for `"2027"` and `"2029"`: `expected` and `drawn`, each with `thirty_hours`, `tax_free_childcare` and `total` as `sd_bn`, `p5_bn`, `p95_bn`; `source_households_30_hours` and `effective_source_households_30_hours` (Kish over their shares of the cost), both rounded to tens. A floor: calibration held fixed, clustering ignored. |
| `budget.age_profile_sensitivity` | One-sided: `even_spread_bn` (the 30 hours' change in `year` with the newly entitled 1-to-4-year-olds spread evenly across those ages), `centax_gradient_bn` (on CenTax's under-3 to 3-4 gradient, the Microcosm team's figure, `source_url`), `share_over_limit_by_age_pct` (children aged 0-4 in families with a parent over £100,000), `babies_bn` (newly entitled age-0 children's cost). |
| `budget.net_bn.total` | Change in the government balance (minus `gov_balance` change), on the expected basis (the expected gross plus the single draw's net-less-gross difference). Equal to gross here: nothing else in the model depends on these limits. |
| `budget.thirty_hours_components_bn` | The `thirty_hours` leg split into `extended`, `universal` and `targeted` (£bn by year, from the claim-all pair times the take-up rate; they sum to `thirty_hours`). On this release `universal` and `targeted` are zero: policyengine-uk 2.147.2 keeps them when a family becomes eligible. |
| `budget.tax_free_childcare_bn` | The Tax-Free Childcare leg by year: `central` (= `gross_bn.tax_free_childcare`), `before_netting` (the reform's top-up on the full pre-reform childcare spending), `take_up_newly_eligible_all` (every eligible family claiming, baseline and reform, netted), `take_up_newly_eligible_low` (that times `take_up_newly_eligible_low_rate`, 0.7: take-up among newly eligible families at 70%, an expectation-basis sensitivity, not in the range). |
| `budget.range_bn` | `low`, `central` (= `gross_bn.total`), `high`, by year. |
| `budget.sensitivities` | `effects_bn`: each adjustment's effect on the gross total by year (+ raises the cost); `low` / `high`: which effects make up each end; `descriptions`. High: `full_30_hour_usage`. Low: `tfc_routed_share` (58% of childcare spending through a Tax-Free Childcare account). Every pair nets the new funded hours off childcare spending in its reform, as the central pair does. |
| `reconciliation` | `concentration[year]`: `gaining_sample_size` (unweighted gaining families), `top_ten_share_pct` and `largest_share_pct` of the shipped static cost, `kish_effective_sample_size` of the gaining families' weights. `parents_over_100k_with_child_under_5_thousands[year]`: adults with adjusted net income (after pension contributions) over £100,000 in a family with a child under 5. `centax_parents_over_100k`: CenTax's ~200,000. |
| `recipients[year]` | `families_gaining` (gain > £1 a year), `children_gaining`, `by_scheme` (**families** gaining from each scheme; a family can gain from both), `mean_gain_gbp` (null when suppressed), `mean_gain_suppressed`, `children_by_scheme`, `children_gaining_by_age` (bands `0-1`, `2`, `3-4`, `5-11`, `12+`), `families_losing` (a model artefact: see METHOD.md). |
| `distribution[year]` | `by_decile`: households by baseline equivalised household net income decile (people-weighted, as policyengine-uk's `household_income_decile`), with `mean_change_gbp`, `pct_change`, `share_gaining_pct`, `suppressed`. `by_region` (the twelve ITL1 regions and nations, in ONS order) and `by_family_type` (lone parent; couple with one, two, or three or more children; other): `total_change_bn`, `families_gaining`, `suppressed`, both on benefit units. Nations are regions here, so there is no separate nation breakdown that could reveal a suppressed region by difference. |
| `gender[year]` | `families_gaining_by_earner`: families gaining by which parent is over £100,000 (baseline adjusted net income): father, mother, both, lone parent; `families_gaining`, `suppressed`, complementary suppression as elsewhere. `partner_not_working`: couples with a child under 12 where exactly one parent is over £100,000, by the sex of that parent: `families`, `partner_not_working_pct` (other parent has no employment or self-employment income). Descriptive and static. |
| `baseline_validation` | Rows (including free-entitlements spending against DfE, central and with full 30-hour usage, 2025-26 and 2026-27) `label`, `year`, `model`, `official`, `unit`, `source`, `url`, `note`. |
| `assumptions` | Keyed `microcosm`: the model's take-up draws (share of families with `would_claim_*` true, overall and where a parent is over £100,000), `reports_childcare_spend_pct` (families with a child under 12 reporting paid registered childcare, overall and where a parent is over £100,000), `uses_tfc_pct` (families with a child under 12 receiving a top-up) and mean extended hours used. |
| `modelling_assumptions` | List of `id`, `title`, `modelled` (what the code models), and for a tested assumption `alternative` and `effect_bn` (by year; the same numbers as `budget.sensitivities.effects_bn`, for `take_up` `budget.tax_free_childcare_bn.take_up_newly_eligible_low` less `central`, or for `static` minus `labour_supply.total_offset_bn.central`). |
| `labour_supply` | The labour supply response (METHOD.md, "Behaviour"); every figure keyed `central` / `low` / `high`, then by year. Blocks `extensive`, `intensive` (`at_or_below_limit` and `over_limit` parts). Offsets are £bn, **positive = money back to the Exchequer**, and apply to the total (not split by scheme). `extensive` (moving into work): `offset_bn`, `tax_and_ni_bn` (the offset before the Tax-Free Childcare top-up on entrants' new childcare), `entrants`, `leavers`, `ftes` (to the nearest 100), `earnings_bn`; diagnostics `implied_by_over_limit`, `non_worker_rule_entrants`, `entry_capped` (entry dropped where a non-worker's probability would exceed 1) and `allocated_by_cell_level` (implied entrants placed among non-workers of the same `sex_couple_child_quintile` cell, or, where it has none, of the coarser `sex_couple_child`, `sex_couple` or `everyone` cell; nearest 100); `entry_sensitivity` (sensitivity, central elasticities, by year, not in the dynamic cost): `same_cell_only` (entry that would be placed in a coarser cell dropped) and `worker_profile` (that entry given the implying workers' weighted median hourly wage at 18.8 hours, gain and subsidy recomputed), each with `offset_m` (£m to 0.1), `entrants`, `earnings_bn`; and `central_offset_m`, the central offset at the same precision. `intensive` (hours of every responding adult in work): `offset_bn` (net, the model's tax and benefits recomputed once on the combined earnings change) = `price_offset_bn` (the marginal-childcare-price effect recomputed alone, ≥ 0) + `income_offset_bn` (the OBR income effect, the remainder, ≤ 0), `ftes`, `earnings_bn`; each of `at_or_below_limit` and `over_limit` has the same three offsets, `ftes`, `price_ftes`, `income_ftes`, `earnings_bn`, and diagnostics `workers`, `workers_paying_for_childcare`, `workers_price_falls`, `workers_fully_covered` (thousands; families whose paid care the newly funded hours fully cover), `mean_price_change_pct` (marginal price, among those paying), `mean_income_change_pct` (the household's gain, paid care displaced by the funded hours plus the change in cash income recomputed at the displaced spend, as a percentage of household disposable income). `intensive_displacement` (sensitivity, central elasticities, both groups): `offset_bn` and `price_offset_bn` by `low`/`high` displacement, and `displacement`. `intensive_income_basis` (sensitivities, central elasticities, both groups, keyed by basis: `paid_care_fixed_spend`, the central gain with childcare spending held fixed, so the cash support on displaced spend is kept; `government_cost`, the gain counting the funded hours at government cost): each `offset_bn`, `price_offset_bn`, `income_offset_bn` by year. `intensive_price_basis` (sensitivities, same shape): `original_spend`, the price effect's reform childcare support rate read at today's spend rather than the spend left after displacement; `tfc_only`, the marginal price net of Tax-Free Childcare alone (the Universal Credit childcare element and other childcare-linked support left out). `total_offset_bn` = extensive + intensive at the same bound; `dynamic_cost_bn` = `budget.gross_bn.total` − `total_offset_bn`. `population[year]`: `responding_adults` (the participation screen, OBR Table A4), `hours_responding_adults` (the hours screen, Table A3, which keeps disabled adults), `adults_moved_outside_population` (the build allows up to 0.2% of `responding_adults`; about 1,300 in 2026-27 and 2027-28 on this release, issue #11). `assumptions` (elasticities, including `income_elasticities`; `couples` and `couples_issue_url`, the OBR groups assigned to married and cohabiting couples alike; 18.8 entrant hours, free-hours displacement), `responding_population`, `not_modelled`, `notes`. |
| `cliff_example` | The published cliff family: `description`, `year` (2027), `earnings`, `net_income_baseline`, `net_income_reform` (household net income minus the family's own fixed childcare spending), `notes`. Read from `household_grid`'s default series (no separate simulation). |
| `household_grid` | The dashboard's household form, precomputed with `calculate_household()`: `year`, `region`, `earnings` (the higher earner's employment income: £1 to £140,001 every £1,000, plus every £100 from £95,001 to £105,001), `options` (`parents`, `children`, `spend_per_child`, each `id` + `label`), `default` (the `cliff_example` family, which hits the cliff) and `series` keyed `parent\|children\|spend` with `baseline` and `reform` net income after childcare spending. |
| `benchmarks` | `source`, `figure`, `year`, `like_for_like`, `url`, `announcement_url`, `underlying_source`, `underlying_source_url`. The reported £0.7bn traces to CenTax (free hours only); the comparable figure is CenTax's static £0.98bn (2030) against our `budget.gross_bn.thirty_hours`, given as `ours` with `difference_from_underlying_static_bn`; `underlying_static_by_year_bn` also gives CenTax's £0.80bn for 2028 (key `"2027"`); `underlying_static_before_bunching_by_year_bn` gives £0.86bn and £1.07bn. |
| `limitations` | Strings. |
